The Free Guide

How to Get Out of Debt — For Real This Time

No get-rich-quick tricks, no shame. Just the seven steps that actually move the number down, in an order that won't burn you out by week two.

Most debt advice fails for one reason: it ignores real life. This guide won't tell you to stop buying coffee. It'll walk you through the same seven steps we talk through at every Debt Free Dinner Club table — in an order that builds momentum instead of guilt.

1

Get Honest About the Full Picture

You can't pay off what you haven't looked at. List every debt in one place — credit cards, student loans, medical bills, buy-now-pay-later plans, money owed to family. For each one, write down:

  • The total balance
  • The interest rate (APR)
  • The minimum monthly payment
  • The due date

A notebook, a spreadsheet, or a free app all work. What matters is that it's all in one place, where you can see it.

This step is uncomfortable for almost everyone. That discomfort is normal — it means you're finally looking instead of avoiding. It gets easier from here.
2

Choose Your Payoff Strategy

Once every debt is listed, pick an order to attack them. The two most proven methods:

  • Debt Snowball: Pay minimums on everything, then throw every extra dollar at your smallest balance first. When it's gone, roll that payment into the next-smallest. Best if you need quick wins to stay motivated.
  • Debt Avalanche: Same idea, but you attack the debt with the highest interest rate first. Best if you want to pay the least amount of interest overall and can stay motivated without quick wins.

There's no wrong answer — the best strategy is the one you'll actually stick with. Many of our members start with the snowball for the momentum, then switch to avalanche once a few small debts are gone.

3

Build a Bare-Bones Budget You'll Actually Keep

You don't need a perfect budget — you need one that survives contact with real life. Try a simple zero-based approach: every dollar of income gets a job (bills, minimum debt payments, groceries, a small amount of fun money, and debt payoff) until you reach zero.

  • Start with needs: housing, utilities, food, transportation, minimum debt payments
  • Add a small, guilt-free "fun money" line — a strict budget with zero flexibility rarely survives a month
  • Send whatever is left to your target debt from Step 2
4

Find Extra Money Without a Second Job (Unless You Want One)

The fastest way to speed up payoff is to widen the gap between income and expenses. Some places to look first:

  • Call your internet, phone, and insurance providers and simply ask for a lower rate — it works more often than people expect
  • Cancel subscriptions you forgot you had
  • Sell things you no longer use
  • If you want extra income, a short-term side gig can shave months off your timeline — but it's optional, not required
5

Lower Your Interest Rates

Every percentage point you shave off an interest rate is money that goes toward your balance instead of a bank. Options to explore:

  • Call your credit card company and ask for a lower APR — especially if you've paid on time
  • Look into a 0% balance transfer card if you can realistically pay it off before the promotional rate ends
  • Talk to a nonprofit credit counseling agency about a debt management plan (see our directory) — these can lower rates and combine payments into one
Be careful with for-profit "debt settlement" or "debt relief" companies. Some are legitimate, but many charge high fees and tell you to stop paying creditors, which can wreck your credit and lead to lawsuits. Always check a company against the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and FTC before signing anything — links in our directory.
6

Protect Yourself From Backsliding

Nothing derails debt payoff like a surprise expense landing on a credit card. Before you go all-in on payoff:

  • Save a small starter emergency fund — even $500–$1,000 covers most surprises
  • Put a pause on new credit card use while you pay down existing balances
  • If a card is a temptation, freeze it (literally or with your bank's app) rather than closing it, which can affect your credit history
7

Track Progress & Celebrate Every Win

Paying off debt is a marathon, and marathons need mile markers. Track your total debt monthly, celebrate every balance that hits zero, and don't do it alone — accountability from other women on the same path is one of the biggest predictors of sticking with a plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will it take to get out of debt?

It depends entirely on your total debt, interest rates, and how much extra you can put toward it each month — there's no universal timeline. What matters more than speed is consistency: a realistic plan you can stick with for 12 months will beat an aggressive plan you abandon after six weeks.

Should I use a debt consolidation or debt settlement company?

"Consolidation" through a nonprofit credit counseling agency's debt management plan can genuinely help by lowering interest rates and combining payments. "Debt settlement" companies that tell you to stop paying creditors are riskier — they often charge large fees, can tank your credit, and don't guarantee results. Research any company through the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and Better Business Bureau first. See our directory for vetted nonprofit options.

What if I can't cover even the minimum payments?

Call your creditors before you miss a payment — many have hardship programs. Then reach out to a nonprofit credit counseling agency (like those listed in our directory); a free counseling session can help you understand every option, including options beyond what's covered in this guide.

Do I need a big income to get out of debt?

No. Plenty of women in this community have paid off significant debt on modest incomes by combining a realistic budget, a clear payoff order, and community accountability. Income helps, but it isn't the deciding factor — your plan and your consistency are.

Snowball or avalanche — which one is actually better?

Avalanche saves more money in interest over time. Snowball tends to keep people motivated longer because of quick wins. The "better" method is whichever one you'll follow for the next year. Many people start with snowball and switch once momentum kicks in.
